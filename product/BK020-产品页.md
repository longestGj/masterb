## FAQ Guide（下半段）

> 以下为一问一答，覆盖黑色母粒的选型、参数、应用、采购、合规与质量问题。每个回答简洁、可执行，帮助你在下单前做出正确判断。

### What is black masterbatch, and what is it made of?

Black masterbatch is a concentrated pellet made of carbon black pigment dispersed in a carrier resin with dispersing aids and additives. Instead of handling dusty carbon black powder, you dose identical pellets into your base resin. Our BK020 contains 20% carbon black, an LLDPE + LDPE carrier, PE wax and stearic acid, and 47.8% CaCO₃ filler, compounded and cut into uniform 2.5mm granules. The carrier makes it compatible with your base resin, while the carbon black delivers the color and UV protection.

### What is carbon black masterbatch, and how is it different from plain black masterbatch?

"Carbon black masterbatch" and "black masterbatch" refer to the same product — the pigment is carbon black (Color Index Pigment Black 7). The name simply emphasizes the pigment. When a supplier says "carbon black masterbatch," they are telling you the black comes from carbon black rather than a dye, which matters because carbon black also gives UV protection and opacity, not just color.

### Why use masterbatch instead of raw carbon black powder?

Raw carbon black is a fine, dusty powder that is hard to handle, hard to dose accurately, and hard to disperse evenly. A masterbatch pre-disperses the carbon black into a pellet, which eliminates dust in your workshop, gives you reproducible dosing, and delivers consistent color batch after batch. In practice, switching from powder to masterbatch usually means a cleaner floor, stable filter pressure, and fewer rejected rolls — which is why processors keep moving to pelletized concentrate.

### What is "jetness" in a black masterbatch?

Jetness is how deep and neutral the black looks. It depends mainly on the carbon black particle size — smaller particles give a deeper, more blue-toned black, while larger particles look browner but are easier to disperse and cheaper. For a deep black in film or molded parts, you want a fine furnace black that is well dispersed. For a cost-driven product like a trash bag, a slightly lower jetness at a lower cost is often the better trade-off than chasing the deepest possible black.

### What is the difference between carbon black content and carbon black grade?

Carbon black content tells you how much carbon black is in the pellet (our BK020 is 20%). Carbon black grade tells you the type of carbon black — its particle size, structure, and surface chemistry. Two masterbatches with the same 20% content can perform very differently if one uses a fine high-jetness grade and the other a coarse low-cost grade. The grade sets the ceiling on how black and how UV-resistant you can get, while the content sets how much you dose. Always check both numbers before you compare suppliers.

### How much carbon black content do I need for my product?

It depends on your application and the final opacity you need. For film and injection molding, 20% to 35% carbon black is a common working range, added at 2% to 5% by weight. For deep black with a low addition rate, a higher content (35% to 50%) lets you dose less. The finished product is what matters — a trash bag needs enough carbon black to be fully opaque, not a specific pellet percentage. Our BK020 at 20% is a balanced, cost-effective choice for trash bags and general molding.

### What does the carrier resin do, and why does it have to match my base resin?

The carrier resin is the bridge that carries the carbon black into your polymer. If the carrier does not match your base resin, you get poor color development, matte surfaces, or reduced impact strength. A mismatched carrier looks fine in the pellet but fails in the part. Our BK020 uses an LLDPE + LDPE carrier, which is directly compatible with LDPE and LLDPE film and molding applications, so it melts and mixes cleanly instead of leaving gels or weak points.

### Can I use a PE-based black masterbatch in a PP product?

Generally yes for simple products, because PE and PP are both polyolefins and share some compatibility. But for demanding PP parts, a PE carrier can lower stiffness or cause surface issues. If you run PP, tell us — we can supply a PP-carrier black masterbatch that matches your resin instead of forcing BK020 into a job it was not built for.

### What is the recommended addition ratio (let-down ratio) for black masterbatch?

The typical let-down ratio is 1% to 5% by weight, depending on your product and the opacity you need. Our BK020 is recommended at 2% to 5%. Adding too little gives a grey or translucent product; adding too much wastes material and can hurt mechanical properties and processing stability. Start with a small-batch trial on your own line to find the exact ratio for your machine, product thickness, and target blackness — then lock it in for repeat runs.

### What does MFI (melt flow index) mean for black masterbatch, and why does it matter?

MFI tells you how easily the masterbatch melts and flows. If the masterbatch MFI is far from your base resin, it will not mix evenly and can cause streaks or poor dispersion, because the two materials travel through the screw at different speeds. Our BK020 has an MFI of 3–50 g/10 min (190°C, 21.6 kg), and we can adjust the MFI to match your machine and resin so it flows the way your line needs. Matching the MFI is what makes the difference between a clean color and visible streaks.

### What is dispersion, and how is it measured?

Dispersion is how evenly the carbon black is separated inside the carrier. Poor dispersion shows up as black specks in film, screen pressure rise, and weak spots in thin parts. It is measured by a filter pressure value (FPV) test — forcing melt through a fine mesh and recording pressure rise over time; a lower, flatter FPV means better dispersion. Good dispersion is the single most important quality factor in a black masterbatch, because it is what you cannot see until the defect is already in your product.

### Why do two masterbatches with the same 30% carbon black perform differently?

Because carbon black content is only one parameter. The carbon black grade, carrier resin, dispersant system, and compounding process all affect the result. A masterbatch with the same 30% content but a cheaper grade and weak dispersion will need a higher addition rate and still look worse, while a well-dispersed grade reaches the target blackness at a lower dose. Judge the final result on your line, not just the percentage on the datasheet — two identical-looking datasheets can be very different products.

### What does moisture content matter for film and injection molding?

Moisture in the masterbatch turns to steam in the barrel and creates bubbles, voids, splay marks, and uneven surfaces. This matters most in thin film. Our BK020 is controlled to 0.2% max moisture, which helps you avoid surface defects. For humid climates, we also pack in moisture-resistant bags to keep the pellets dry in storage.

### Does my black masterbatch contain filler (CaCO₃), and how does that affect me?

Yes, our BK020 contains 47.8% CaCO₃ filler, which lowers the cost per kilogram while maintaining a consistent black. The trade-off is that filler can slightly reduce gloss and mechanical strength compared to a filler-free grade. For cost-driven products like trash bags and utility parts, this is usually a good deal — you get an opaque black at a lower cost per ton of output. If you need a higher-gloss or higher-strength black, tell us — we can supply a grade with less or no filler instead.

### What is the best black masterbatch for trash bags / garbage bags?

For trash bags, you want a cost-effective grade with good opacity and clean dispersion, because films are thin and any agglomeration shows up as specks. Our BK020 is built exactly for this — a 20% carbon black, LLDPE/LDPE-carrier grade that gives dependable black for refuse bags and carrier bags at a low cost per bag.

### What black masterbatch should I use for blown film?

For blown film, dispersion and low gel count are the priorities, because the film is thin and any agglomeration shows up as a visible defect. You also want a carrier matched to your film resin and low moisture to prevent bubbles. Our BK020 uses an LLDPE + LDPE carrier with stable dispersion and 0.2% max moisture, making it a solid choice for blown film lines making garbage bags, carrier bags, and shopping bags.

### Which black masterbatch is suitable for injection molding?

For injection molding, you want uniform color, good surface finish, and a carrier that does not reduce the mechanical strength of the molded part. Flow marks and weld-line weakness are the common failures when the masterbatch does not match the resin. Our BK020 disperses cleanly with no carbon black spots, and its adjustable MFI lets us match the flow to your molding machine. It works well for general black molded parts like household items and industrial components.

### Can I use black masterbatch for recycled plastic / re-granulating?

Yes. Black masterbatch is widely used to color recycled resin, and black is ideal for masking the natural color variation of regrind — one of the main reasons converters pick black for recycled PE. Our BK020 is suitable for re-granulating and recycled PE, and its CaCO₃ filler helps offset the cost of recycled material. Because recycled resin varies from lot to lot, we recommend a trial to confirm the addition ratio and final blackness on your specific regrind before you commit.

### What is the best black masterbatch for LDPE / LLDPE film?

For LDPE and LLDPE film, the carrier must match the film resin so it melts and mixes cleanly. Our BK020 is formulated with an LLDPE + LDPE carrier, so it is directly compatible with your LDPE/LLDPE film line. This matched carrier gives clean dispersion and consistent color without gels or streaks.

### What black masterbatch should I use for HDPE pipe (UV protection)?

For HDPE pipe that needs UV protection, the carbon black grade and dispersion matter more than the blackness. Pipe grades typically need about 2% to 2.5% carbon black in the finished pipe, finely dispersed. BK020 is a general-purpose grade for bags and molding — if you need a dedicated pipe grade with UV-stable carbon black, contact us and we will recommend the right product.

### Why does my film show black specks / tiger stripes?

Black specks and tiger stripes are almost always a dispersion problem — carbon black agglomerates that were not fully broken down during compounding, or a carrier that does not match your film resin. In blown film, even a small number of undispersed agglomerates becomes visible as the film is stretched thin. A well-dispersed masterbatch like our BK020 avoids this. If the problem started after switching suppliers, the new grade likely has weaker dispersion, and a higher dose will not fix it — you need a better-dispersed grade.

### Why does my black product look grey instead of deep black?

Four usual causes, in order: total carbon black content too low (dosage × concentration), a coarse pigment grade, poor dispersion leaving light-scattering agglomerates, or color variation in your natural resin. Check your addition ratio first, then the grade and dispersion. If it is still grey, contact us — we will help you diagnose it and recommend the right grade and dosage for your product.

### How should I compare the price of two black masterbatches?

Do not compare price per kilogram alone. Compare the cost per ton of your finished product, using "masterbatch price × actual addition rate." A cheaper masterbatch that needs a higher dose can cost you more per ton of output. Also factor in dispersion — poor dispersion forces you to add more to reach the same blackness, which erases the price advantage. The real question is what it costs to make one ton of your product at an acceptable blackness, not what one kilogram of masterbatch costs.
