S-02

What does a sample trial need to prove?

A trial is usually judged on four things: colour or function target against your control sample, dispersion and appearance in your own process, stability across the shots or metres you run, and whether anything changed in the downstream product.

Agreeing the target before the trial starts is what makes the result usable.

Reading a data sheet

T-01

What is pigment loading, and how does it differ from the addition ratio?

Loading is how much pigment or carbon black sits inside the masterbatch itself. The addition ratio is how much of that masterbatch you feed into your process. They are related but not the same thing: a higher-loading grade is usually dosed at a lower ratio.

Neither figure can be assumed from a colour name. Both are confirmed against the specific grade.

Confirmed against the exact grade
T-02

What do MFI or MFR tell me on a masterbatch sheet?

MFR (melt flow rate) and MFI (melt flow index) describe how much resin flows in ten minutes under a set temperature and load. On a masterbatch sheet the number refers to the carrier resin, not to the pigment.

Flow affects how the grade melts and disperses in your process. A figure is only meaningful next to the test conditions printed with it, so compare sheets on conditions before comparing numbers.

Answered in full on this page
T-03

Is a typical value the same as a specification value?

No. A typical value is what the grade has generally shown; it is not a committed limit. A specification value is a range the grade is expected to hold, and it is what acceptance should be measured against.

Both depend on the test conditions stated in the file. If a sheet only lists typical values, there is no published limit to accept or reject against.

Answered in full on this page
T-04

What is a control sample, and why does it matter?

A control sample is material that produced a result you already accepted. You keep it and run a new batch side by side with it, on the same resin, the same machine and the same settings.

That turns “is this the right colour?” into “does this match the control?”, which is a question a line can actually answer. It is also how batch-to-batch variation gets judged.

Answered in full on this page
T-05

What are screen pack blockage and a filtration test?

Screen pack blockage is when the filter screens in the melt path clog faster than expected, so pressure rises or screens have to be changed sooner. A filtration test passes a set volume of melt through a screen and measures what it takes to reach a defined pressure.

Both are used as an indication of undispersed material or gel content in a grade. What they show depends on your screen size, throughput and the grade being tested, so results are read per setup rather than as a fixed pass mark.

Read against your screen size and setup

A-02

Does the base resin matter?

Yes. Carrier and base resin compatibility affects selection, dispersion and the properties of the final part. Please share the resin you process, such as PE, PP, LDPE, LLDPE or HDPE.

Answered in full on this page
A-03

Can one grade cover several products?

It can, but it is not something to assume. A grade that runs well in blown film may behave differently in injection molding because of residence time, shear and cooling, even on the same resin.

If you want one grade across several lines, send the resin and the process for each product so the review can look at them together.

Reviewed across each process you run
