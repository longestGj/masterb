How to Choose Carbon Black Content in Black Masterbatch

A 40% grade is not automatically better than a 20% grade, and a higher percentage does not always lower cost. Start with the application, the blackness target and the cost per ton.

Carbon black content is not the final result

Carbon black content is the percentage of pigment inside each pellet. A 40% grade carries twice as much pigment per pellet as a 20% grade, so it should require fewer pellets to reach the same blackness. That number alone still does not tell you what the finished product will look like.

01

Carbon black grade

Particle size and structure influence whether the black reads deeper and more blue-toned or browner and more economical.

02

Carrier resin

The carrier has to match the base resin. A mismatch can produce weak spots and poor color development.

03

Dispersion quality

Poor dispersion can leave black specks and grey patches even when the stated loading is high.

Compare the whole formulation.

Two grades with the same percentage can perform differently when the carbon black, carrier or dispersion changes.

20%–40%: what each band is for

20–25%

Cost-led production

Lower loadings suit thick parts and general molding where the absolute blackness target is less demanding. These grades usually carry more calcium carbonate filler and run at a higher addition rate.

Use for
Cost-sensitive trash bags, re-granulating, thick molded parts and recycled resin.
Watch
More filler can slightly reduce gloss and mechanical strength. A low dose can also make the black look grey.
30%

The general-purpose midpoint

The 30% range balances blackness, filler level, addition rate and price. It is a practical starting point for many bags, films and general molded parts.

Use for
General trash bags, injection molding and packaging film.
Watch
The grade still needs to match the resin and process; the midpoint is not universal.
35–40%

Higher blackness at a lower dose

Higher loadings carry less filler and support deeper, higher-gloss black at a lower addition rate. The price per kilogram is higher, but the cost per ton of finished product can be lower when dosage falls enough.

Use for
Premium film, high-gloss molded parts and thin gauge with a strong opacity target.
Watch
Confirm feed stability and dispersion at the final addition rate.

Higher loading is not always cheaper

A higher percentage helps only if the pigment stays well dispersed and the actual addition rate falls. A cheaper grade that needs 5% addition can cost more per ton than a higher-loading grade that runs at 2%.

Actual material cost Masterbatch price × actual addition rate Then include dispersion, feeding stability and changeover effects.

Price per kilogram is a starting number. Cost per ton of finished product is the purchasing decision.

Filler lowers cost and changes the surface

Calcium carbonate lowers cost per kilogram, but it occupies volume that would otherwise be polymer. Across the BK range, filler falls as carbon black content rises.

Pick a starting grade by process

Trash and garbage bags

20%–40%

Economy bags start with BK020 or BK030. Premium bags can move to BK035 or BK040.

Blown film

BK025 / BK035 / BK040

Thin gauge makes dispersion visible, so confirm the film carrier and test the final addition rate.

Injection molding

BK020–BK035

Match color uniformity, surface finish and flow. BK040 is the high-gloss direction.

Recycled PE

BK020

Black helps mask regrind variation. Confirm dosage and final blackness on the actual feedstock.

Confirm four inputs before ordering

  1. 01
    Base resin and process

    PE, PP or another polymer, plus film, injection, blow molding or re-granulating.

  2. 02
    Blackness and gloss

    Define the appearance target and whether calcium carbonate filler is acceptable.

  3. 03
    Addition rate and cost

    Choose a target let-down ratio and compare material cost per ton of finished product.

  4. 04
    Dispersion and sample

    Ask for filter pressure data and test the grade on your own line.

Choose by product, then compare cost

There is no single best carbon black content. Balance blackness, dosage and cost for your product, then verify the grade with a line trial. A useful supplier should recommend a loading for your process, not only sell the grade already in stock.
