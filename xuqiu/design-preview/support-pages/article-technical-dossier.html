01 / Definition

The material in one paragraph

Masterbatch is a concentrated mixture supplied as pellets or another solid form. It is combined with a base polymer during processing so that a colorant, additive or filler can be distributed through the finished material.

It is a delivery format. The useful question is not only “what is masterbatch?” but “what does this grade contain, what carrier does it use, and what process is it intended for?”

02 / Composition

Three roles, many possible formulations

The carrier polymer helps the concentrate combine with the base resin. The active content provides the intended effect. Additional components may support dispersion, stability or processing.

03 / Selection

What the buyer needs to provide

A reviewable requirement is specific enough to connect material, process and result.

  • Base resin and current product reference.
  • Process and processing conditions available at this stage.
  • End product, appearance target and current issue.
  • Quantity range and document requirements.

04 / Evidence

Separate the document from the claim

A TDS is technical product information. An SDS is safety and handling information. A COA is tied to a batch. A test report covers the sample or scope named in that report.

Before using a document in a customer project, confirm that the product identity, application and regulated market match the scope under review.

Buyer questions

Before you request a grade.

Is masterbatch the same as a pigment?

No. A pigment may be one component. A masterbatch is a formulated delivery system made for a polymer process.

Can I compare products by color alone?

No. Compare carrier, active content, target process, technical data and the final application requirement.

Which details start a review?

Resin, process, end product, target, current grade or reference, quantity range and document needs.
