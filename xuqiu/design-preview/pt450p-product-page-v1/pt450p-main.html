01Product position and food-grade scope

What Is Black Masterbatch PT-450P?

PT-450P is a pelletized black masterbatch from GE CHEMICAL that carries 42% P-type virgin carbon black in a 57.8% virgin LLDPE carrier with no filler. You dose the black granules into your base resin instead of handling dusty carbon black powder. The grade is built for film blowing and injection molding, where you want high pigment loading, clean dispersion, and a concentrate that does not dilute the polymer package with CaCO₃.

What Is a 42% Carbon Black Masterbatch, and Why Does Loading Matter?

A 42% carbon black masterbatch means nearly half the pellet mass is pigment. Higher loading usually means stronger tint strength per kilogram of concentrate, so you can reach target opacity at a controlled let-down ratio. PT-450P sits at 42% virgin carbon black (P Type), which places it in the high-loading end of GE CHEMICAL's black range. Loading alone does not replace a line trial, but it is the first commercial lever when you compare filled low-pigment grades with premium no-filler concentrates.

What Does "P Type Carbon Black" Mean on the PT-450P TDS?

The TDS lists carbon black as 42% P Type / virgin carbon black — P Type. In supplier language, that labels the pigment family used in this grade. We do not invent particle-size jetness grades or ASTM labels beyond that sheet. For buyers, the practical takeaway is that PT-450P is specified as a P-type virgin carbon black concentrate; you should judge final blackness, gloss, and opacity on your own film or molding trial rather than assuming a universal jetness class from the letter alone.

How Is PT-450P Different From PT-300?

PT-300 is a filled cost-aware grade: 25% carbon black, 41.8% CaCO₃, LLDPE + LDPE carrier, and applications that include film blowing, injection molding, and re-granulating. PT-450P is a high-loading no-filler grade: 42% P-type virgin carbon black, 57.8% virgin LLDPE, without filler, Food Grade Yes, and applications listed as film blowing and injection molding. Choose PT-300 when filled economics and regrind coloring matter most; choose PT-450P when you want higher pigment density, zero filler in the concentrate, and the food-grade flag on the product data.

How Does PT-450P Compare With BK040?

BK040 is a premium 40% carbon black grade with about 5.8% CaCO₃ still present, aimed at high jetness film and molding. PT-450P sits at 42% P-type carbon black with a true without-filler design and virgin LLDPE carrier, plus Food Grade Yes on the TDS. If your brief is "near-premium loading but zero filler and food-grade listing," PT-450P is the closer match. If you already standardize on the BK040 recipe and only need 40% with low filler, compare both on a side-by-side trial rather than switching by percentage alone.

Why Choose a No-Filler Black Masterbatch Instead of a Filled Grade?

Without filler means the concentrate mass is pigment plus carrier (and whatever process aids are built into the compound), not limestone. That can help gloss, mechanical balance, and pigment efficiency versus a heavily filled pellet. Filled grades like PT-300 remain smarter for high-volume cost control and many regrind jobs. PT-450P is for lines that want the pigment and polymer bridge to dominate the bag — especially when buyer specs push away from CaCO₃ in the masterbatch itself.

Is PT-450P a Food Grade Black Masterbatch?

Yes, according to the TDS: Food Grade is listed as Yes. That makes PT-450P our food-grade black masterbatch option in the product data sense. We do not invent named third-party certificate numbers or specific regulation citations that are absent from the sheet. If your customer file requires a food-contact declaration, migration support, or market-specific paperwork, ask for the current TDS plus the lot documents your QA team needs, and we will support qualification with what we can supply for this grade.

What Should Buyers Ask for When Qualifying a Food-Grade Black Masterbatch?

Ask for the current TDS that shows Food Grade Yes, a lot COA for carbon black content / moisture / MFI, SDS handling information, and any declaration your market or brand owner requires. Do not assume a silent FDA or EU number on the website equals a filed certificate. For PT-450P, start from the product data flag, then build the buyer file with documents you actually receive and approve — that is safer than marketing claims that outrun the paperwork.

02Carrier, dosage and flow

What Carrier Resin Does PT-450P Use, and Why Virgin LLDPE?

PT-450P uses 57.8% virgin LLDPE as the carrier. The carrier is the polymer bridge that melts into your polyethylene base resin and carries the carbon black with it. A virgin LLDPE carrier suits many LLDPE and related PE film and molding recipes because it is designed to mix inside a normal PE process window. High carrier share plus zero filler keeps more of the pellet as polymer rather than mineral, which is part of the no-filler value story.

Can I Use an LLDPE-Carrier Black Masterbatch Like PT-450P in a PP Product?

For simple PP parts you may get acceptable color, because PE and PP are both polyolefins. For demanding PP moldings, a PE carrier can soften the part or hurt surface quality. If your base resin is PP, tell us before you lock PT-450P — we can discuss a PP-carrier option rather than forcing an LLDPE-carrier grade into a job it was not written for on the datasheet.

Why Does Carrier Match Matter as Much as the 42% Carbon Black Number?

Carbon black percentage sets how much pigment sits in the pellet. Carrier match decides whether that pigment develops cleanly in your melt. A mismatched carrier can leave streaks, gels, or dull surfaces even when the datasheet shows a strong carbon black number. PT-450P's virgin LLDPE carrier is written for PE film blowing and injection molding, so the percentage and the carrier work together instead of fighting your resin.

What Is the Recommended Let-Down Ratio for PT-450P?

The TDS recommends 2%–5% by weight, or an adjustment based on your actual production. Too little leaves grey or translucent product; too much wastes concentrate and can affect flow and mechanics. Start inside 2%–5% on a short trial, check opacity and filter pressure, then lock the ratio for repeat runs. Because PT-450P carries 42% carbon black with no filler, many lines reach target opacity inside that window without jumping dosage as aggressively as with a filled low-pigment grade.

How Should I Decide Between 2% and 5% Addition on My Line?

Use the lower end when film is thicker, natural resin is already dark, or opacity looks strong early in the trial. Move toward the upper end for thin film, lighter natural resin, or parts that need extra masking. Always judge by finished blackness and defect rate, not by copying another plant's percentage. PT-450P's high loading often allows a lower practical dose than a 20–25% filled grade for the same visual target — confirm that on your gauge and mold, not on a spreadsheet alone.

What Does MFI Mean for Black Masterbatch PT-450P?

MFI (melt flow index) describes how easily the masterbatch melts and flows under a set load and temperature. For PT-450P, the TDS lists 3–50 g/10 min at 190°C and 21.6 kg, adjustable to demand. If masterbatch MFI sits far from your base resin, the two materials can travel through the screw at different speeds and leave streaks. Matching MFI to your machine and resin is a practical way to keep color uniform on film and injection lines.

Can GE CHEMICAL Adjust the MFI of PT-450P for My Machine?

Yes. The TDS states that MFI can be adjusted as per your demand inside the published working idea of 3–50 g/10 min at 190°C / 21.6 kg. Send your base resin MFI, process temperature, and whether you blow film or inject. We then align the pellet flow so PT-450P mixes cleanly instead of forcing you to fight streaks with higher dosage alone.

What Does "Good Dispersion, No Carbon Black Spot" Mean on the PT-450P TDS?

It means the carbon black is broken down and held in the carrier so you should not see discrete black speckles in film or molded surfaces under normal processing. Poor dispersion shows up as spots, tiger stripes, or rising screen pressure. PT-450P's TDS rates dispersion as good with no carbon black spot, which matters even more at 42% loading because thin film and glossy molded faces make every agglomerate visible.

What Does "Good Softening, Do Not Block the Filter" Mean for Production?

Softening describes how cleanly the concentrate melts and passes screen packs without building pressure from undispersed pigment or hard residues. PT-450P is rated good for softening and stated not to block the filter. On a film or injection line, that translates to more stable filter life and fewer unplanned screen changes when you stay inside normal process windows and keep moisture under control.

Why Do Two High-Loading Black Masterbatches Perform Differently on the Same Line?

Because content is only one number. Carbon black type, carrier type, filler level, compounding quality, and moisture all change the result. One 40–42% grade can look deep and clean; another can look grey and speckled at the same dose. Judge PT-450P by a line trial — opacity, filter pressure, surface, and any food-grade documentation needs — not by matching percentage alone on two datasheets.

03Quality, filler and applications

Does PT-450P Contain CaCO₃ Filler?

No. The TDS states Without filler. That is a core difference versus filled neighbors such as BK020, PT-300, and even BK040 (which still carries a small CaCO₃ share). If your specification or brand owner rejects mineral filler in the black concentrate, PT-450P is the GE CHEMICAL option written for that brief.

What Is the Best Way to Use PT-450P as a Black Masterbatch for Film Blowing?

Match the LLDPE carrier to your PE film resin, start inside the 2%–5% let-down range, keep bags sealed so moisture stays near the 0.2% max control, and check opacity across the roll. Confirm that screen pressure stays stable — the TDS softens as "do not block the Filter." For shopping bags, industrial film, or general black blown film where you want high loading without filler, PT-450P is built as a black masterbatch for film blowing rather than a pipe-only UV specialty.

Is PT-450P Suitable as a Black Masterbatch for Injection Molding?

Yes. The TDS lists injection molding as a primary application. You want uniform color, clean surfaces, and flow that matches the molding resin. PT-450P's adjustable MFI, good dispersion claim, 0.2% max moisture, and no-filler design support general black molded parts where gloss and pigment efficiency matter. Validate weld lines and flow marks on a short shot trial, then lock dosage.

Is PT-450P Listed for Re-Granulating Like PT-300?

No. The PT-450P TDS applications are Film Blowing and injection molding. Re-granulating is listed on PT-300, not on PT-450P. You can still discuss recycled PE coloring with us, but if regrind economics and filled masking are the main job, start with PT-300 or another filled grade rather than assuming PT-450P is the regrind workhorse.

How Much Carbon Black Ends Up in the Finished Part When Using PT-450P?

Multiply the masterbatch carbon black content by the let-down ratio. At 42% carbon black and 3% addition, the finished compound carries about 1.26% carbon black from the masterbatch (0.42 × 0.03). At 4% addition, that estimate is about 1.68%. Those are planning numbers only — opacity also depends on film thickness, pigment type, dispersion, and any color already in the base resin. Use the estimate to plan trials, then confirm visually and with your QC method on the actual product.

What Moisture Level Does PT-450P Target, and Why Does It Matter?

Moisture is controlled to 0.2% max on the TDS. Excess moisture turns to steam in the barrel and creates bubbles, voids, splay, or uneven film. That risk rises in humid storage or when bags stay open. Keep PT-450P in sealed 25 kg bags, reseal between uses, and pre-dry if your plant climate is very humid. Moisture control is as important as carbon black percentage for clean film and molded surfaces.

How Is PT-450P Packaged?

PT-450P is packed in 25 kg bags. Use intact bags, store them dry, and avoid leaving opened bags exposed overnight. Consistent packaging helps protect the 0.2% max moisture target during shipping and warehouse storage. If you need a different pallet layout for your forklift or silo feeding system, raise that when you request a quote so packing can be discussed before shipment.

What Granule Size Does PT-450P Use, and Does Size Matter?

The granule size is 2.5mm × 2.5mm × 2mm. Uniform pellets feed more evenly through volumetric or gravimetric dosing and reduce bridging compared with irregular chips. Size alone does not guarantee dispersion quality, but consistent granules make dosing repeatable — which is what you need when you lock a 2%–5% let-down ratio for film or molding.

Do not stop at price per kilogram. Calculate cost per ton of finished product: masterbatch price × actual addition rate. What Causes Black Specks or Tiger Stripes When Running Black Masterbatch?

Specks and tiger stripes usually come from poor dispersion, carrier mismatch, or unstable dosing — not from "not enough masterbatch" alone. Raising dosage will not erase agglomerates; it can even make streaks worse. PT-450P is rated for good dispersion with no carbon black spot, so if defects appear after a grade change, compare dispersion and carrier first. Also check for contamination, worn screens, and moisture-related surface noise that can look like pigment defects.

Why Does My Black Film or Part Look Grey Instead of Opaque Black?

Common causes include addition rate too low for the gauge, incomplete dispersion that scatters light, a lighter natural resin, or comparing a filled low-pigment habit against a high-loading grade without resetting dose. Check your let-down ratio inside the 2%–5% window first, then confirm dispersion and film thickness. PT-450P's 42% no-filler loading often solves mild greyness versus a weaker filled grade, but a line trial is still required before you change the whole plant.

What Causes Bubbles or Voids When Processing PT-450P?

Moisture is the first suspect. Steam forms in the barrel and leaves bubbles, voids, or splay. Keep PT-450P near the 0.2% max moisture control by storing sealed 25 kg bags in a dry area. In humid seasons, consider a short pre-dry step and avoid leaving hoppers open. Also rule out wet base resin, because moisture problems are not always coming from the masterbatch alone.

How Should I Store Black Masterbatch PT-450P?

Store bags indoors, off the floor, away from rain and steam, and keep them sealed until use. Reseal opened bags promptly. Good storage protects the moisture specification and keeps pellets free-flowing for dosing. Avoid long outdoor storage in tropical ports without cover. Stable storage is a cheap way to protect the quality you paid for in a high-loading black masterbatch like PT-450P.
